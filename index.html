Wiki AfroDemics Project — Content & Gender Gap Tracker

Make the record even

Curating knowledge. Elevating African voices. Advancing equitable representation. That is the mission of the Wiki AfroDemics Project, and this dashboard brings that mission to life through live data. Every statistic displayed here is retrieved directly from Wikidata when the page loads and refreshed automatically every few minutes, no manually updated figures, no cached data. Add a new biography of an African academic or scientist today, and you will see the impact reflected almost immediately.

Select any African country to discover academics and scientists who are documented on Wikidata but are still missing a Wikipedia article in English, French, or the country's official-language edition. The dashboard also highlights where content gaps are higher including the gender disparities that the Wiki AfroDemics Project is working to address.

Developed for the Wiki AfroDemics Project, this dashboard serves as a real-time companion for participants, editors, and the wider Wikimedia community, transforming contributions into measurable impact.

Continental overview

Scientists & academics per country, article coverage in English and French, and the gender split — refreshed automatically.
How this stays fast: instead of one giant SPARQL query joining all 54 countries, this fires 54 small, single-country queries, a few at a time, and streams each row in the moment it lands — so the table fills in live instead of making you wait on the slowest country.

Occupation matching: a person counts if their occupation is scientist, researcher, academic, or university teacher — or any subclass of those (e.g. "linguist," "biologist," "historian," "professor of X"), via Wikidata's subclass-of (P279) chain. This catches people whose occupation statement is more specific than the four broad classes, at the cost of a slower per-country query — a country with a lot of items can occasionally time out and need the inline retry button below.

Country detail

Full breakdown including the country's local official-language Wikipedia, gender per person, and the actual names of people with no article anywhere.
Data: Wikidata Query Service, queried live via SPARQL, 54 countries in parallel. Occupation match: scientist, academic/university teacher, or researcher (P106) — including any subclass of these via wdt:P279*; nationality via P27; gender via P21. "Local wiki" is an approximate single official-language mapping per country for now — several countries (Cameroon, Rwanda, Chad, etc.) have more than one official language and would need a small refinement to check all of them.
